Hedgerouter reads the metadata your AI gateway already emits and produces a dollar exposure ledger: what you spend, on which models and providers, and how much of that spend moves with compute prices rather than with your own usage. Then it prices the instruments that could cap it, and reports how much of your real cost line each one tracks.

Compute is a commodity. Your budget treats it like a subscription.

Companies shipping AI have taken on a commodity position nobody priced. Four things are true at the same time, and they push the same direction.

  1. 01

    GPU-hours have a spot price

    Rented accelerator capacity reprices week to week and public indices already quote it. If you buy on demand, you hold that price risk with no cap and no forward.

  2. 02

    Token prices are a floating rate

    Providers cut list prices, raise them, deprecate models, and move your mix underneath you. A rate card is the price you pay this quarter, not a fixed cost.

  3. 03

    Usage grows with the product

    Every agent you ship raises consumption. Volume growth and price moves compound, and the usual remedy is to make the product worse.

  4. 04

    Nobody holds the position in dollars

    Finance sees one invoice a month. Engineering sees tokens per request. Neither is an exposure, and you cannot bound what nobody has written down as a dollar position.

The first deliverable is the position, not a trade. Most teams we have looked at cannot answer "how many dollars of next quarter's AI spend is price, and how many are volume?" That number is computable from telemetry they are already emitting.

From gateway telemetry to a bounded cost line

Hedgerouter is not a model router. Keep whatever gateway chooses and serves your models. Hedgerouter reads its output and prices the exposure underneath it.

  1. 01

    Ingest

    NDJSON exports, JSON access logs, OTLP spans, Claude Code transcripts. Nothing new to instrument, and replaying the same export twice is idempotent.

  2. 02

    Normalize

    Model, provider, token counts, realized cost, latency, status, failover. The boundary is an allowlist, so prompts, paths, tool arguments and identities are never read in.

  3. 03

    Position

    A daily ledger in dollars and tokens by model, provider and route, with the price component separated from the volume component.

  4. 04

    Price

    Public compute indices and listed contracts, quoted at prices you could have transacted at, with fees included. No midpoints nobody could have traded.

  5. 05

    Size

    A hedge is sized against a stated budget with a per-event cap, and never larger than the exposure it covers. Sizing runs before anything is placed, not after.

  6. 06

    Report basis

    What fraction of your actual cost line the instrument tracks, as a number in every run. When the fit is poor, the output says so.

Where this risk is becoming tradeable

A hedge is only as good as the instrument under it. Here is what exists, and what each one actually settles on.

Compute-price indices

closest to a real cost line

Published GPU-hour indices move with what you pay for rented capacity. For a team buying accelerator time on demand this is the exposure itself, not a proxy for it, which is why it is the arm we instrument first.

Liquid compute markets

forming

Spot and forward trading in compute capacity is being built out now. Once capacity has a forward curve, an AI budget can be run the way an airline runs fuel: buy the term, cap the peak, stop guessing.

Provider list-price contracts

settles on the price you pay

These settle on a provider's own published price page. For a customer paying list, the settlement variable is not correlated with your cost, it is your cost. Tenors are annual, so the first settlements land in 2027.

Blended $/token indices

weak fit for a single buyer

A volume-weighted blend across labs. Most of its variance is the market's model mix changing, not price. A buyer with a stable mix has little exposure to it, and the tool reports that instead of routing you into it.

A hedge on the wrong variable costs premium and leaves the risk in place, so basis is a reported number in every run rather than an assumption in a pitch.

What runs today, and what doesn't

Being specific here is cheaper than being caught out later. The measurement half is real and you can run it this afternoon. The execution half is not built.

Runs today

  • Ingest from Weave Router exports, JSON access logs, OTLP spans and Claude Code transcripts
  • Metadata-only normalization behind an allowlist boundary
  • Daily dollar and token exposure ledger by model, provider and route
  • Basis reported against public compute-price indices

In progress

  • Forward pricing against compute venues as they list capacity
  • Hedge sizing against a stated budget and per-event cap, evaluated offline
  • Recurring reports finance and engineering read from the same artifact

Not built

  • No order execution. Hedgerouter does not connect to a broker and places no trades.
  • No model routing. It stays out of your request path.
  • No prompt, completion or content storage, at any tier.

Your telemetry never has to leave your infrastructure

Hedgerouter is self-hosted and normalization is an allowlist, not a log archive. The fields below are not filtered after the fact. They are never read into the ledger in the first place.

Read the boundary spec
  • Prompts and completions
  • Code, paths and filenames
  • Tool arguments and tool output
  • Credentials and API keys
  • Email addresses and gateway user IDs
  • Session IDs, pseudonymized with a rotating local salt
  • Telemetry upload, off by default
  • delete-data --confirm DELETE removes the ledger and identity
