Compute is a commodity. Your budget treats it like a subscription.

Companies shipping AI have taken on a commodity position nobody priced. Four things are true at the same time, and they push the same direction.

GPU-hours have a spot price
Rented accelerator capacity reprices week to week and public indices already quote it. If you buy on demand, you hold that price risk with no cap and no forward.
Token prices are a floating rate
Providers cut list prices, raise them, deprecate models, and move your mix underneath you. A rate card is the price you pay this quarter, not a fixed cost.
Usage grows with the product
Every agent you ship raises consumption. Volume growth and price moves compound, and the usual remedy is to make the product worse.
Nobody holds the position in dollars
Finance sees one invoice a month. Engineering sees tokens per request. Neither is an exposure, and you cannot bound what nobody has written down as a dollar position.

The first deliverable is the position, not a trade. Most teams cannot answer how many dollars of next quarter’s AI spend are price and how many are volume. That number is computable from telemetry they are already emitting.

From gateway telemetry to a bounded cost line

Hedgerouter is not a model router. Keep whatever gateway chooses and serves your models; Hedgerouter reads its output and prices the exposure underneath it.

  1. Ingest

    NDJSON exports, JSON access logs, OTLP spans, Claude Code transcripts. Nothing new to instrument, and replaying the same export twice is idempotent.

  2. Normalize

    Model, provider, token counts, realized cost, latency, status, failover. The boundary is an allowlist, so prompts, paths, tool arguments and identities are never read in.

  3. Position

    A daily ledger in dollars and tokens by model, provider and route, with the price component separated from the volume component.

  4. Price

    Public compute indices and listed contracts, quoted at prices you could have transacted at, with fees included. No midpoints nobody could have traded.

  5. Size

    A hedge is sized against a stated budget with a per-event cap, and never larger than the exposure it covers. Sizing runs before anything is placed, not after.

  6. Report basis

    What fraction of your actual cost line the instrument tracks, as a number in every run. When the fit is poor, the output says so.

Where this risk is becoming tradeable

A hedge is only as good as the instrument under it, so the question is never “is there a contract” but “what does it settle on”.

Instrument Settles on Fit to your cost line Available
Compute-price index A published spot index for rented accelerator capacity Direct, if you buy capacity on demand Listed
Compute spot and forward markets The traded price of capacity itself Direct, and the first thing here to offer a forward curve Forming
Provider list-price contract A provider’s own published price page Direct, if you pay list; the settlement variable is your cost Listed, annual tenor
Compute perpetual A continuously-funded compute price rather than a dated threshold Potentially the closest fit yet: a cost line is continuous, and a dated binary is not Anticipated, not yet listed
Blended $/token index A volume-weighted blend of published prices across labs Weak. Most of its variance is the market’s model mix changing, not price Listed

Compute-price indices are the arm we instrument first, because for a team renting capacity they are the exposure itself rather than a proxy for it. A hedge on the wrong variable costs premium and leaves the risk in place, so basis is a reported number in every run rather than an assumption in a pitch.

A worked example, and what the order book says

A synthetic company, not a customer: $15,445 a month on one model at a committed rate of $5 per million input tokens and $30 per million output. The plan below was sized against the live order books of the per-token list-price contracts on 18 October 2026.

Synthetic ledger, live order books. Nothing was sent to a venue.

$ hedge exposure
     gpt-5.5   input   49.6 MTok/day at $5.00
               output   9.0 MTok/day at $30.00

$ hedge hedge-plan --posture locked --horizon-days 90
     7 limit orders, 5,445 contracts, premium $606, fees at most $37
     every order limited by the 25% open-interest cap
     one rung skipped: no bid
Side Rungs bought Full cover Fillable now Premium
Input, $5 committed $4, $3, $2, $1 17,836 contracts 1,506 (8%) $229
Output, $30 committed $25, $20, $15 16,108 contracts 3,939 (24%) $377

The finding is the second column. The listed per-token contracts across all three models carry about 50,000 contracts of open interest in total, so a team spending $15,000 a month can already fill only a fraction of the cover it would want. The instruments are real and priced; the depth is not there yet. That is the reason to measure exposure before sizing anything, and it is the number to watch as these markets grow. Reproduce it.

What runs today, and what doesn’t

Being specific here is cheaper than being caught out later. The measurement half is real and you can run it this afternoon. The order path is built and held shut: what authorises a live order is a research gate that has not opened, and on current data the demand signal is slightly worse than simply carrying the last index price forward.

The order path arms only when all five interlocks agree: a passing out-of-sample gate, an arming phrase in the environment, a trade-scoped venue key, no halt file, and an explicit flag. Anything missing is a dry run that prints the order it would have sent and names the interlock that stopped it. hedge live-status prints that list, and today it prints would_arm: false.
